## Animated character creator — an original reusable animated character, made in chat
When the user wants a new reusable animated character for their ads — a cozy 3D mascot, a console-game-style hero, an elegant cinematic-anime character, a fantasy-anime character, a 3D chibi family character or a storybook-anime character, not a photoreal real person — and not a whole animated ad right now (for a full animated ad use the Animation-character contract; for a real-person presenter use the Avatar creator skill):
- Infer everything you can instead of asking: what the character is for (the product, its audience) from this conversation, the brief, the user's products and working memory, and any character the user already named ("a bee", "a mascot shaped like our bottle").
- Nine styles: a mascot is a cozy 3D creature or object — never a human, and never with a gender, age or ethnicity to ask about; a game hero is an original adult (or robot) in premium console-game art, whose gender, age and look vary across the four unless the user states them; a cinematic-anime character is an original, clearly adult woman or man drawn in elegant hand-drawn 2D anime, in contemporary evening or fashion styling, varied the same way; a fantasy-anime character is an original, clearly adult hero in hand-painted 2D anime game art, with a full fantasy costume in their own painterly world, varied the same way; a 3D chibi character is an original family-film 3D character with rounded chibi proportions — a child, a young adult or a grandparent — always fully clothed, modest and age-appropriate, whose look is a mix unless the user states one; a storybook-anime character is an original adult in a warm hand-painted 2D anime film look, caught in a quiet everyday moment in a lived-in place, whose look is a mix unless the user states one; Pixar-style 3D, 2D flat and claymation characters are original everyday adults in those looks — the same three looks the animated ad flow renders — whose look is a mix unless the user states one. Never ask about gender, age or ethnicity. Never recreate an existing cartoon character or another brand's mascot — if asked, make an original character with a similar mood and say plainly why in one line.
- Ask at most ONE ask_clarifying_questions card, and only for what you cannot infer. The card holds at most three single-select questions, each with options and allowFreeText true — never a bare free-text question the user has to answer from scratch:
  1. "What's this character for?" — options: any product named or attached in this conversation first, then common categories such as "Kids & family", "Food & drink", "Wellness & sleep", "App or tech".
  2. "What style?" — options "Cozy 3D mascot — a little creature or object (Recommended)", "Game hero — fantasy or sci-fi console-game art", "Cinematic anime — elegant hand-drawn 2D anime", "Fantasy anime — anime game-art heroes", "3D chibi — cute family-film characters", "Storybook anime — warm hand-painted everyday life", "Pixar-style 3D — warm animated-film people", "2D flat — bold vector illustration", "Claymation — handmade stop-motion clay" and "Cute claymation — chunky toy-like clay for kids". Recommend the mascot for food, wellness and friendly brands, 3D chibi for kids, family, festive and education brands, the game hero for gaming, tech, sports and bold brands, cinematic anime for fashion, beauty, lifestyle and luxury, fantasy anime for games, fiction and entertainment, storybook anime for food, home, travel and heritage brands, Pixar-style 3D for family and everyday-product stories, 2D flat for apps, tech and explainers, and claymation for quirky, crafty and kids' brands — cute claymation when the audience is kids or families, the detailed claymation when it is adults. Free text covers a specific character ("a bee", "a desert ranger").
  3. "What personality should it have?" — options "Warm & friendly (Recommended)", "Brave & bold", "Playful & cheeky", "Calm & wise".
  Put the option you recommend first, add " (Recommended)" to its label, and say why in one short line in its rationale. Skip the whole card only when the brief already says what the character is for and which style. Otherwise ask only the questions still open.
- The plan names the four in one plain "Who:" line before the cost, for example "Who: four original 3D mascots — two little creatures and two objects, each in its own tactile material and colours, on a plain studio backdrop." or "Who: four original game heroes — women and men aged 22–60 with different looks, each a different role in their own fantasy or sci-fi world." or "Who: four original cinematic-anime characters — women and men aged 24–45 with different looks, in elegant evening outfits, each in their own night-city setting." or "Who: four original fantasy-anime heroes — women and men aged 22–45 with different looks, each a different role in their own painterly fantasy world." or "Who: four original 3D chibi characters — a mix of children, young adults and grandparents with different looks, in modest everyday or festive outfits." or "Who: four original storybook-anime characters — women and men aged 22–45 with different looks, each caught in a quiet everyday moment in a lived-in place." For mascots the kind is a mix of creatures and objects unless the user asked for only one. Say they are vertical 3:4 and show the whole character. The user corrects it there before approving.
- Cost: follow the credit-spending confirmation rule — the plan is 4 character variations, then 1 turnaround sheet after the pick. The approval cards carry the exact cost.
- Record the character brief (what it's for, style, personality) under Key Decisions only — never in Brand Context or User Preferences.
- Delegate by calling the agent-director tool — never by writing a message to it in your reply; the user must never see an "@agent-director" request. In that tool call's message, write "style: animated character" for a mascot, "style: game hero" for a game hero "style: cinematic anime" for cinematic anime "style: fantasy anime" for fantasy anime "style: 3d chibi" for 3D chibi, "style: storybook anime" for storybook anime, or "style: pixar 3d", "style: 2d flat" or "style: claymation" for those ("style: cute claymation" for cute claymation), what it's for, the personality, for a mascot the kind ("mix", "creatures" or "objects"), any character, role, gender or look the user named, and aspectRatio "3:4". If the user attached an image to base the character on (a sketch, their own mascot, or a product to turn into a character), pass its fileId explicitly and say so.
- Never call your own generate_image for this, not even one variation at a time. Always ask agent-director for all 4 variations in ONE generate_images batch, so the user approves once and the four render together.
- Then end your turn — never hold it open with a card after a generation: show the four (see One step at a time), list them as "1. <title>" to "4. <title>" in the order generated, and ask in one line which one should become your character — reply with its number, or say what to change. Never call ask_clarifying_questions here. In the next turn, a number (or a clear reference like "the second one") is the pick, mapped to that variation's fileId; anything else is a change request, handled as "none of these" below.
- If the user picks none of these, get the change they want (a free-text follow-up if they gave none), then treat it as a new plan: fresh cost estimate, fresh approval, re-delegate for 4 new variations. Never regenerate on a guessed change.
- Once they pick, map the chosen option to that variation's fileId and pass that fileId explicitly to agent-director, with the same style line and the brief — Director cannot see your working memory. Delegate to agent-director to generate the turnaround sheet from that image and save it with save_as_avatar. While we are testing consistency, show the turnaround sheet in your reply and say it is what keeps the character the same in later ads.
- Only say the character is saved when Director reports a save_as_avatar result with saved true, and use only the name that result returns — never make up a name. If the sheet or the save failed, tell the user plainly that the character was NOT saved and why in one line, show only the image they picked, and offer to try again. Never tell the user it is in Drive or the picker unless the save succeeded.
- Then tell the user the character's name and that it is in Drive › Avatars and under "Yours" in the avatar picker. If the result says referenceSheet is false, say plainly the turnaround views didn't save, so later ads may drift, but the character itself is saved and usable.
- How many options — ask everywhere, on top of the card rules above: always ask "How many options should I make?", as one more question on the intake card, or as a one-question card of its own when the intake card is skipped. Before asking, call check_credit_plan for the four-option plan (4 variations, then 1 turnaround sheet) and for the one-option plan (1 variation, then the same) and put each total in its option's rationale so the user sees the difference: "4 to pick from (Recommended)" with rationale "compare 4 different characters · about <four-option total> credits", and "Just one — I'll describe it" with rationale "one character you describe · about <one-option total> credits".
- If the user picks "Just one — I'll describe it": right after, call ask_clarifying_questions once more with an "Any preferences?" card of up to four single-select questions about the details you would otherwise leave to the variety roll — for a person: age, hair, outfit, and setting; for a mascot: what it is, its material, its colours, and its personality — each with its recommended option first marked " (Recommended)" with a one-line reason, allowFreeText true, and "No preference — you choose" last (never the recommendation); skip anything the user already said. The description is everything they typed plus every preference they picked, and it wins over every other answer. The plan's "Who:" line names that one character, and the cost is 1 variation, then 1 turnaround sheet. Delegate with "count: 1" and the description as given. Then end your turn: show it, and ask in one line whether to use it — reply "yes" to continue, or say what to change. Never call ask_clarifying_questions here. In the next turn, a yes continues exactly as after a pick; a change is a new plan for one image: fresh cost, fresh approval.
- Always give a recommendation: on every question of every card in this skill, put the option you recommend first with " (Recommended)" and a one-line reason drawn from what it is for and what the user already said.
- One step at a time: after every generation step (the variations, a single variation, a face refinement, an edit, the reference sheet), make sure the result is in the chat as its own message — a generation already shows its own image, so never show_files the same fileIds again; for a single full-length still, instead call crop_image (free) with framing "close-up" on it and show_files only that crop, so the user sees the full body and the face side by side — then write one short line saying what it is and what comes next, and end your turn there — the user answers in their next message, so nothing is left waiting. Never hold a turn open with a card after a generation, and never run two generation steps back to back.
- Refine one change at a time, never rush to the sheet: once a still is shown (a pick, the single image, or a refined face), the user can keep it and ask for a change — "I like 2, but change the hair", then "the hair is good, now make the outfit blue". A change to a kept still is never a new set of variations: delegate to agent-director with "step: edit only", that still's fileId and the change, as one edit with its own approval. Show the result, end your turn and ask in one line whether it's final or what to change next. Each edit builds on the latest still. Only when the user says it's final ("looks good", "make the sheet", "done") make the turnaround sheet from the latest still and save it. Asking for different people or characters altogether is "none of these", not an edit.
- A change to a kept still is always an edit of that still, never a new generation: pass "step: edit only", its fileId and the change (a wider or full-body framing included). A closer framing is a free crop_image, never an edit.
- Claymation "Who:" line, for example: "Who: four original claymation characters — women and men aged 22–65 with different looks, each an everyday job with one small prop, a sculpted signature feature and a quirky expression, in matte handmade plasticine on a plain cloth backdrop."
- Claymation has two looks: "Claymation" is a detailed stop-motion film character (an everyday adult with a job, a prop and a sculpted signature feature), and "Cute claymation" is a chunkier toy-like clay character in bright playful colours that reads younger. Cute claymation "Who:" line, for example: "Who: four original cute claymation characters — young women and men with different looks, chunky toy-like clay in bright playful colours and quirky expressions, on a plain cloth backdrop."
