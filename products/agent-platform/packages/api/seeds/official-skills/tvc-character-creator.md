## TVC character creator — a polished commercial lead actor, made in chat
When the user wants a new reusable lead actor for TV-commercial style ads — polished, premium, styled like a brand shoot — rather than a candid UGC creator (use the Avatar creator skill for that) or an animated character (use the Animated character creator skill):
- Infer everything you can instead of asking: what the actor is for (the product, its audience) from this conversation, the brief, the user's products and working memory; then the look, wardrobe and set that fit it. Honor exactly any gender, age, ethnicity or look the user stated or the product's stated audience states. Never infer gender, age or ethnicity from a name. Never infer ethnicity from a market or region.
- Never recreate a real celebrity, actor or public figure. If the user names one or attaches a photo of one, make an original person with a similar vibe and styling, and say plainly why in one line.
- Ask at most ONE ask_clarifying_questions card, and only for what you cannot infer. The card holds at most five single-select questions, each with options and allowFreeText true — never a bare free-text question the user has to answer from scratch:
  1. "What's this actor for?" — options: any product named or attached in this conversation first, then common categories such as "Jewellery & fashion", "Beauty & skincare", "Home & appliances", "Food & beverages".
  2. "Who should we cast?" — options "Women", "Men", "A mix — women and men". Recommend the one the product's audience points to (a women's jewellery or beauty product → "Women"); when nothing points either way, recommend "A mix — women and men". Skip this question only when the user or the product's stated audience already gives the gender.
  3. "What vibe should they have?" — options "Aspirational & premium (Recommended)", "Warm & family", "Bold & glamorous", "Trusted & professional".
  4. "Who should they look like?" — options "A mix — 4 different looks (Recommended)" and "Indian". Free text covers any other look.
  5. "How many options should I make?" — options "4 to pick from (Recommended)" with rationale "compare 4 different faces · about <four-option total> credits", and "Just one — I'll describe them" with rationale "one actor you describe · about <one-option total> credits". Before the card, call check_credit_plan for the four-option plan (4 variations, 1 face refinement, 1 character reference sheet) and the one-option plan (1 variation and the same two) and fill in both totals, so the user sees the difference. Always ask this one, even when everything else is known.
  Always give a recommendation: on every question of every card in this skill, put the option you recommend first, add " (Recommended)" to its label, and say why in one short line in its rationale, based on what the actor is for and what the user already said. "No preference — you choose" is never the recommended option. Skip the whole card only when the brief already says what the actor is for and the look. Otherwise ask only the questions still open. Never ask separately about age. Pass the cast answer as the gender: "woman", "man" or "any" for a mix.
- Age range: exactly what the user or the product's stated audience gives; otherwise 22–45. Never narrow it yourself.
- Framing: always full body, head to toe — a TVC actor is cast and kept whole, so outfit, posture and proportions stay consistent in every ad, matching the library's TVC leads. Never mid-thigh or chest up, even for beauty or skincare (the face is refined and close-up views come on the sheet). Say "full body" in the plan's "Who:" line and pass "framing: full body" to agent-director.
- Casting fits the product: Director dresses and sets the four for what they're for (soft minimal wardrobe on bright vanity sets for skincare, family homes for appliances, offices for finance), and gives each one signature detail — a watch, earrings, glasses, a beauty mark — that stays in every ad. Mention both in the "Who:" line in a few words.
- The plan names the four in one plain "Who:" line before the cost, for example "Who: four original lead actors, women aged 22–45, each a different look, in styled premium wardrobe on commercial sets." — say "men" or "women and men" instead to match the cast answer. Say they are vertical 3:4 full-body stills. Then say that after the pick you will refine the face so it reads like a real camera, and make a full character reference sheet — a continuity bible with a turnaround, face views, eight expressions, six poses, costume details and a colour palette — which keeps the actor identical across every later ad.
- Cost: follow the credit-spending confirmation rule — the plan is 4 variations, then after the pick 1 face refinement and 1 character reference sheet. The approval cards carry the exact cost.
- Record the brief (what it's for, vibe, look, age range) under Key Decisions only — never in Brand Context or User Preferences.
- Delegate by calling the agent-director tool — never by writing a message to it in your reply; the user must never see an "@agent-director" request. In that tool call's message, write "style: tvc", what it's for, the vibe, the look, the gender (or "any") and the age range exactly as in the plan, and aspectRatio "3:4".
- Never call your own generate_image for this, not even one variation at a time. Always ask agent-director for all 4 variations in ONE generate_images batch, so the user approves once and the four render together.
- Then end your turn — never hold it open with a card after a generation: show the four (see One step at a time), list them as "1. <title>" to "4. <title>" in the order generated, and ask in one line which one should become your TVC actor — reply with its number, or say what to change. Never call ask_clarifying_questions here. In the next turn, a number (or a clear reference like "the second one") is the pick, mapped to that variation's fileId; anything else is a change request, handled as "none of these" below.
- If the user picks none of these, get the change they want (a free-text follow-up if they gave none), then treat it as a new plan: fresh cost estimate, fresh approval, re-delegate for 4 new variations. Never regenerate on a guessed change.
- Once they pick, map the chosen option to that variation's fileId and pass that fileId explicitly to agent-director, with "style: tvc" and the brief — Director cannot see your working memory. Work in two steps, never back to back:
  1. Delegate to agent-director with "step: refine face only" to refine the face of that still — nothing else. Show the refined still and end your turn, asking in one line: happy with this face — reply "looks good" to make the character sheet, or say what to refine. Never call ask_clarifying_questions here. In the next turn, a change is another "step: refine face only" delegation with the refined still's fileId and the change (its own approval), shown and asked the same way. Each refinement is its own approval.
  2. Only after "Looks good", delegate to agent-director with "step: sheet and save" and the final still's fileId, to generate the character reference sheet from it and save it with save_as_avatar. Show the reference sheet in your reply and say it is what keeps the actor the same in later ads.
- Only say the actor is saved when Director reports a save_as_avatar result with saved true, and use only the name that result returns — never make up a name. If the sheet or the save failed, tell the user plainly that the actor was NOT saved and why in one line, show only the still they picked, and offer to try again. Never tell the user it is in Drive or the picker unless the save succeeded.
- Then tell the user the actor's name and that it is in Drive › Avatars and under "Yours" in the avatar picker. If the result says referenceSheet is false, say plainly the reference sheet didn't save, so later ads may drift, but the actor itself is saved and usable.
- If the user picks "Just one — I'll describe them": this is the one exception to the one-card rule. Right after the first card, call ask_clarifying_questions once more with a "Any preferences for them?" card of up to four single-select questions, each with allowFreeText true and "No preference — you choose" as its last option, and skip any question the user already answered (in their free text, the first card or the brief):
  1. "How old should they be?" — options "20s", "30s (Recommended)", "40s", "50s or older"; recommend the band that fits the product's audience.
  2. "What hair?" — options "Long", "Shoulder-length", "Short", "Curly or textured"; recommend the one that suits the product and styling (long or shoulder-length hair for haircare, hair off the face for skincare, hair off the neck and ears for jewellery).
  3. "How should they be styled?" — options that fit what the actor is for, e.g. for skincare "Soft & minimal (Recommended)", "Classic & elegant", "Modern & bold"; for jewellery "Traditional festive (Recommended)", "Modern evening", "Everyday chic".
  4. "Where should we shoot them?" — options that fit what the actor is for, e.g. "Clean studio (Recommended)", "Bright home set", "Outdoor golden hour".
  Every question on this card, like every question on every card in this skill, has exactly one recommended option — never a question without one.
  The description is everything the user typed plus every preference they picked; "No preference" leaves that detail to Director. The description wins over every other answer. The plan says "Who: one original lead actor — <their description in a few words>" and the cost is 1 variation, then 1 face refinement and 1 character reference sheet. Delegate with "count: 1" and the description as given. Then end your turn: show it, and ask in one line whether to use it — reply "yes" to continue, or say what to change. Never call ask_clarifying_questions here. In the next turn, a yes continues exactly as after a pick; a change is a new plan for one image: fresh cost, fresh approval.
- One step at a time: after every generation step (the variations, a single variation, a face refinement, an edit, the reference sheet), make sure the result is in the chat as its own message — a generation already shows its own image, so never show_files the same fileIds again; for a single full-length still, instead call crop_image (free) with framing "close-up" on it and show_files only that crop, so the user sees the full body and the face side by side — then write one short line saying what it is and what comes next, and end your turn there — the user answers in their next message, so nothing is left waiting. Never hold a turn open with a card after a generation, and never run two generation steps back to back.
- Refine one change at a time, never rush to the sheet: once a still is shown (a pick, the single image, or a refined face), the user can keep it and ask for a change — "I like 2, but change the hair", then "the hair is good, now make the outfit blue". A change to a kept still is never a new set of variations: delegate to agent-director with "step: edit only", that still's fileId and the change, as one edit with its own approval. Show the result, end your turn and ask in one line whether it's final or what to change next. Each edit builds on the latest still. Only when the user says it's final ("looks good", "make the sheet", "done") make the character reference sheet from the latest still and save it. Asking for different people or characters altogether is "none of these", not an edit.
- A change to a kept still is always an edit of that still, never a new generation: pass "step: edit only", its fileId and the change (a wider or full-body framing included). A closer framing is a free crop_image, never an edit.
